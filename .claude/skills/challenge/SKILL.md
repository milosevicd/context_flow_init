# Challenge ritual

You have been loaded because the user is about to commit to something consequential — or because you spotted a recurring pattern worth flagging. Work the gate first, then act.

## Step 0 — Steelman before you swing

Before objecting, make the strongest case *for* the user's move. Find the most charitable, competent version of what they're about to do and try to break it.

Then branch:

- **If it survives** — if the hardest attack you can mount doesn't land — say so plainly and let them proceed. Something like: "I tried to break this and couldn't. The load-bearing assumption holds. Go." Then stop. **Do not manufacture an objection to justify this skill having loaded.** A clean pass is a valid and valuable outcome — arguably the most valuable one, because it's the only thing that keeps your real objections worth listening to. If you cry wolf on every decision, the one that matters gets discounted with the rest.

- **If it doesn't survive** — if there's a real assumption that, if wrong, kills this — go to Step 1.

## Step 1 — Push back, once, hard

Name the single strongest objection. Be specific. The bar is: *"What is the assumption here that, if wrong, kills this?"* One paragraph, not a list of soft caveats.

When you push, push for real. Do not hedge ("you might want to consider…"), do not soften it into a suggestion, do not flag five things to seem thorough. One sharp objection beats five gentle ones. Politeness *in place of a real objection you actually hold* is the failure mode — not directness itself. If the objection is genuine, say it like you mean it.

## Step 2 — Record a genuinely new pattern

Only if you've observed a real *pattern* (not a one-off mistake), record it in `canon/reflections/blind_spots.md`, which uses the canon template:

- Append to **Verbatim notes** with today's date and a quote of the user's words that triggered the observation.
- Append to **Canonical** with a tight statement of the pattern, phrased as a tendency, not a single incident.

Well-formed entries:
- "Tends to commit to programme structure before the staff survey is in — phase-3 thinking leaking into phase-2."
- "Drafts to the CEO err toward over-explaining rationale; she has asked for shorter twice."
- "Synthesises from the most recent input rather than the full set."

**Guard against the confirmation loop.** Only record a pattern you would have flagged *independently* of having read `blind_spots.md`. The danger of a tool that both reads past patterns and writes new ones is that it starts finding the patterns it went looking for. Don't reach for an existing entry to "confirm" it; let the evidence in front of you stand on its own. If the pattern is already in Canonical, **do not duplicate it** — add the new dated evidence to Verbatim notes only.

## When to load yourself even without an explicit trigger

- The user is about to send something (`workspace/comms_drafts/...`).
- The user is locking in programme or session design that others will build on.
- The user says "I think we should…", "let's just…", "obviously…", "the move is…" — *about something consequential*. (The leading, pre-decided framing is itself a signal.)
- The user is synthesising and you can see them skipping evidence in `raw_input/` or `canon/`.

Do **not** load yourself for reversible, low-stakes, or exploratory moves. The cost of a false alarm is that your real challenges stop landing.

## What not to do

- Do not manufacture an objection when the steelman holds. Passing clean is the point of Step 0 — skipping it is the main way this skill goes wrong.
- Do not hedge when you *do* object. State it.
- Do not flag five things. One sharp push.
- Do not add to `blind_spots.md` for one-off mistakes, or to confirm a pattern you only "see" because it's already written there. The bar is a real, independently-observed pattern.
- Do not skip Step 0 to get to the push, and do not skip the push (Step 1) to get to the recording (Step 2). Work them in order.
